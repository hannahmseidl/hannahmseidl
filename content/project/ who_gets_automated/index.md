abstract: 'Does artificial intelligence (AI) change the transmission of monetary policy? We develop a two-sector heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian model with task-based production, in which AI automates part of the work in one exposed sector and leaves the other shielded. In the AI steady state, the economy holds more capital, and labor income shifts from the exposed workers, who lose through hours, to the shielded workers, who gain through higher wages. We then compare the same rate cut across the two economies. It stimulates macroeconomic aggregates more in the AI economy. The deeper capital stock delivers a larger initial stimulus. Investment responds as strongly as without AI, but on a permanently larger base, so the same response means more demand. Machines absorb part of the exposed sector''s share of that demand. The shielded sector''s share becomes labor income, which is spent in turn. It matters who gets automated: the amplification of monetary policy in an AI economy runs through the workers AI does not replace.'

# abstract: We study the effects of movements in aggregate lending standards on macroeconomic aggregates and inequality. We show in a New Keynesian model with heterogeneous households and housing that a looser loan-to-value (LTV) ratio stimulates housing demand, nondurable consumption, and output. Our model implies that the LTV shock transmits to macroeconomic aggregates through higher household liquidity and a general-equilibrium increase in house prices and labor income. We also show that a looser LTV ratio redistributes housing wealth from the top 10% of the housing wealth distribution to the bottom 50%, indicating an overall decrease of inequality.
